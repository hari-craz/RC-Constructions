# Phase 15A: release candidate

Frames are in `docs/review/phase-15a/` (`before/` is the build at the start of the phase, `after/` the build at the end; contact sheets `sheet-<size>.jpg` show every section at 390x844, 360x640 and 768x1024). Nothing here says the site is approved or signed off: it lists what was changed, what was measured, what an independent reviewer found, and what is still weakest.

### Flaky tests

The baseline's WebKit failures and two of the final run's (the section-order test and the 390 px morph test on WebKit) waited on timeouts. They now wait on real signals: the section-order test waits for the sheets to be rendered; the morph tests' `walkTo` waits until the published morph value matches the scroll position and two frames have been drawn. Firefox emitted "This site appears to use a scroll-linked positioning effect" in one run of the 14D console test; that is a Firefox advisory about the design (smooth scroll and a scrubbed building), appears only at times, and is now ignored in that one test with the reason written beside it. Run alone or with two workers, all of these pass.

## Full suite

`pnpm test:e2e`, all five projects (chromium, Mobile Chrome, chromium-reduced-motion, firefox, webkit; 1,135 tests, 4 workers), run after the last code change (`docs/review/phase-15a/e2e-final2.log`): **803 passed, 331 skipped (tests that apply to other projects), 1 failed**. The failure was the 14C focus-ring test on chromium-reduced-motion, which read the outline while the browser was still easing it in (3 px and no offset instead of 2 px and 2 px). The test now polls until the computed style settles instead of reading it at once; the whole 14C file then passed on all five projects, and the focus-ring test passed three more times on all five. The full suite was not run again after that test-only change. The earlier full run, before the review-driven changes (strip on one row, bars under text, dimension labels over the house), had 801 passed and 3 failed (two WebKit flakes and the Firefox advisory above), which were fixed as described.

## What is still weakest

1. Text over the building on phones is calmer but not clean: ghost wall fills still sit behind the words, and thin bars show at the edges of text blocks.
2. The mapping of the process copy to the building stages is loose: steps 01 to 03 (visit, estimate, approvals) are drawn as site and foundation work, step 04 ("Mobilisation") is where the frame rises. It is by order and screen position, not word for word.
3. Founder and the contact portrait look unfinished until real photos arrive; wide screens leave large empty areas there.
4. The sample projects carry plausible places, years and areas; the "sample" note is one small line.
5. Header: split light and dark over the sheets; the labels "Principles" and "Track record" differ from the section names.
6. Dimension lines can now cross the house at some angles; roof undersides have no detail.
7. Phone Work delivered hides most lit floors behind plates.
8. Not measured: real-device behaviour (twist gesture, address bar, frame rate), and Phase 14E's anti-aliasing crack count and independent review are still open.
